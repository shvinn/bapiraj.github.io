Logistic regression is the simplest model that still teaches almost everything:
a linear boundary, a nonlinear squashing function, and gradient descent.

## The model

It predicts the probability of the positive class by passing a linear score
through the sigmoid:

$$
p = \sigma(\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{x} + b), \qquad \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}
$$

Training minimizes the binary cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big[y_i \log p_i + (1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)\big]$ by gradient descent.

## What to notice

- A **too-high** learning rate makes the loss bounce or diverge.
- A **too-low** rate converges slowly — many epochs for little progress.
- The boundary is always a straight line: logistic regression is linear in
  feature space. Curved boundaries require feature engineering or a nonlinear
  model.

This same training loop — forward pass, loss, gradient, update — is the kernel
inside every deep network.
